<p>
	<strong>Enterprise Home Lab</strong> is a two-node <strong>Proxmox VE 9.2</strong> cluster
	(<code>proxmox-cluster</code>) that hosts the services I rely on day to day and the Windows domain
	topology I use for systems administration practice. Workloads are split across bare-metal Dell
	PowerEdge hosts — one tuned for density and ZFS storage, the other for identity and lighter VMs —
	linked over a bonded 10G backbone.
</p>

<h2>What's Running</h2>

<p>
	The cluster inventory below mirrors the live Proxmox tree: Linux containers for always-on app and
	data services, and full VMs for the Active Directory / desktop lab.

<h2>What It's For</h2>

<ul>
	<li><strong>Self-hosted services</strong> — Immich, Ghostfolio, Blocky DNS, and the PostgreSQL/Redis/Docker stack that backs them</li>
	<li><strong>Active Directory practice</strong> — dual DCs, member servers, RDS, and domain-joined workstations</li>
	<li><strong>Storage &amp; backup drills</strong> — ZFS on <code>host-01</code> plus rclone cloud mounts for off-site copies</li>
	<li><strong>Automation &amp; IaC</strong> — a safe place to break and rebuild with Ansible, Terraform, and CI pipelines</li>
</ul>

<h2>Future Plans</h2>

<p>
	I'm documenting the full lab as Infrastructure as Code. Once ready, provisioning scripts, network

</p>
